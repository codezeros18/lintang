**Context.** IFEST 2026 — a national 24-hour hackathon on the theme “Tech for Human Connections”. Result: 4th-place finalist.

**The problem.** People considering work abroad receive job offers as posters, brochures and WhatsApp screenshots. The person carrying all the financial risk has no way to check what was promised before paying. They don't need a scam detector — they need a polite reason to wait: *“It's not that I don't trust you, but these points are still unclear.”*

**My role.** Team leader.

**What we built.** **Lembar Janji**: upload a picture of a job offer (or type it), and get one shareable image showing what the offer states, what is still unanswered under Indonesia's Migrant Worker Protection Law (UU 18/2017) with article numbers, and ready-to-ask questions. No account, no install, no personal data. Ten completeness checks, a match against the list of 526 licensed placement companies, Indonesian and Javanese, and 417 tests — including tests that enforce the rule that the product never accuses anyone.

**One lesson.** We had been filtering ideas with a “could ChatGPT do this?” gate. The competition asked for originality — not copied — which is a different thing. A rule we invented for ourselves nearly killed the idea that went to the final. Now I audit every self-made gate against the real rubric: kill switch, or just one weight?
